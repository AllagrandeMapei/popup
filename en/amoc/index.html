Science in the North Atlantic

AMOCthe Atlantic conveyor belt

The AMOC (Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation) is a vast system of currents that moves water between the surface and the depths of the Atlantic Ocean. Warm waters flow at the surface towards the north, cool down between Greenland and Norway, sink because of the density they gain as they cool, and return south along the seabed. The AMOC distributes heat and nutrients through the whole water column of the Atlantic and between north and south, and helps regulate the climate, bringing mild, temperate conditions to Europe. Several studies look at how global warming affects the system and the resulting risk that it weakens; the debate remains open, however, on the timing and scale of the changes. At 26.5°N, the Route du Rhum fleet will sail over the RAPID instrument array, which has been measuring the AMOC since 2004.
